# Listing — Talkwer 1.0.0

## Title

Talkwer — Travel & Tour Showcase Next.js Template

## Description

Talkwer is a dark editorial travel frontend template for agencies, tour
showcases, and destination publishers. Photography-led layouts, detailed
itinerary presentations, and alternate home and destination designs provide a
starting point for a custom travel website.

Built with Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS v4. The source
uses feature folders with page sections and content separated for customization.
English, Spanish, and Japanese interface dictionaries are included; some
editorial copy and metadata remain English.

Included designs and UI examples:

- Three home designs: editorial travel, triptych showcase, and nocturnal
  observatory.
- Three destination listing/detail layouts.
- Tour listings, tour itineraries, story listings, and long-form story pages.
- About, contact, experiences, FAQ, careers, press, partners, and sample policy
  pages.
- Three login/register designs and sample admin/customer dashboards.
- Date/time selection and browser-session booking/checkout demonstrations.
- GSAP/Motion transitions, responsive image layouts, locale routing, metadata,
  and sitemap.
- Offline English HTML documentation, dependency notices, and a self-hosted
  Inter font.

**Important purchase details:** this is frontend source, not a WordPress theme,
CMS, booking service, or backend application. Authentication, payment
processing, availability checks, email delivery, and database persistence are
not included. Contact opens an email draft. Newsletter shows validated demo
feedback without subscribing visitors. Dashboard actions demonstrate sample UI
state.

**Preview images are not included.** The buyer ZIP contains dimension-matched
neutral image placeholders. The Talkwer icon is included. Supply your own
properly licensed photography and branding. Reviews, business figures,
itineraries, observatory measurements, pricing, and policy copy are illustrative
content.

Requires Node.js 22+, pnpm, and Next.js-compatible hosting. It is not a plain
static HTML download. Full source customization requires React/TypeScript
familiarity.

Author: **s0vers — Sovers Tonmoy Pandey**.

## Presentation research (October 6, 2026)

Sources: the 16 most popular Next.js and 16 most popular travel site templates
on Envato Elements, plus ThemeForest item pages for Chisfis (best-selling travel
Next.js template, 1.3K sales) and Resonance (best-selling Next.js 16 creative
template, 1.2K sales), and the four newest Next.js site templates.

- ThemeForest shows a **590×300** main preview on the item page and in search;
  every item checked uses it. The 3:2 cover is what Envato Elements shows.
- Nearly every cover is composed, not a raw screenshot: product name, one-line
  category, small tech badges, two or three short claims (e.g. "3 Home
  Designs"), and a collage of page screenshots, often with a phone.
- Gallery and description images frame real pages in browser windows on branded
  panels, each with a short heading ("10 Unique Design Concepts", "100+ Inner
  Pages"). Descriptions are mostly a stack of these images, about 600 px wide on
  the page.
- Search results show three short feature lines per item; buyers scan these
  before opening an item.
- Travel competitors are overwhelmingly bright (white, teal, blue). Talkwer's
  dark editorial presentation is a point of difference; Resonance shows a dark
  presentation sells.
- Envato guidance: show the item itself, keep cover text minimal, no marketing
  slogans or bundle mentions.
- The tech stack is shown as logo icons (Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind
  CSS), as competing listings do. Logo shapes come from Simple Icons (CC0); the
  marks remain trademarks of their owners and are used only to indicate the
  technology the template is built with.
